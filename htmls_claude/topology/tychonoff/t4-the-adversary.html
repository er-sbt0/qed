Tychonoff · scene IV — why sequences cannot reach it

Pick any subsequence you like. The adversary picks one coordinate and it alternates forever.

Here is the space {0,1}[0,1] — one coordinate for every real number in the unit interval. Tychonoff says it is compact. The sequence fn(x) = the n-th binary digit of x lives in it, and no subsequence converges. Choose your rows; the adversary will answer with a column. You cannot win, and it takes about two seconds to find out.

the constructionGiven your rows n₁ < n₂ < …, set the nk-th binary digit of x to k mod 2 and every other digit to 0. Then fnₖ(x) reads 0, 1, 0, 1, … by construction.
what it does not showThe space is still compact — it has cluster points in abundance. What it lacks is any way to reach them along a sequence.
why countable is differentIn {0,1}ℕ the diagonal argument works and sequences suffice. The theorem's real weight sits in uncountable products, where no picture of the space exists.
the honest omissionNets and filters are what replace sequences here. They are not drawable either, which is worth saying out loud rather than faking.
